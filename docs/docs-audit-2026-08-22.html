  • The documentation gates are narrow but meaningful. They directly protect README.md, docs/command-builder.html, and docs/runner-quickstart.html. They do not establish that every dated review or handoff still describes current code.
  • Several summaries are snapshots, not current maps. The project-summary family predates the 2026-08-22 ACP work. The 2026-08-10 Starlark worker evaluation is explicitly old-contract evidence after the later worker-summary/retry changes recorded in CLAUDE.md.
  • Historical HTML/Markdown twins are inconsistent in authority. CLAUDE.md explicitly says the Safari future-directions Markdown is authoritative and its HTML is derived/truncated. Elsewhere, twin authority is often implicit, so uncertain pairs remain in place.
  • Root clutter is mostly handoff history. Most old handoffs still have inbound historical pointers. The archive nomination is intentionally conservative: only isolated completed handoffs, unreferenced one-off reports, obvious post-run notes, and unreferenced derived presentations are selected.

Trajectory — Codex’s independent reading

The document trail shows a project that has changed centers of gravity three times without abandoning its earlier safety lessons. It began as a local bridge and authentication experiment, became a deliberately small Anthropic-native coding-agent runner, and then grew an unusually evidence-heavy laboratory around permissions, redaction, crash recovery, ledgers, compaction, deterministic orchestration, and model/worker evaluation.

The newest arc is not “more runner features” in the abstract. It is turning the runner into a provider-shaped component that a user-owned interface can drive. ACP (Agent Client Protocol) is the translation boundary; T3 Code is the first real host; provider instances are becoming explicit authority scopes. In that architecture the bridge remains transport/authentication plumbing, the runner remains the policy-and-tool loop, ACP exposes a stable host contract, and T3 supplies the human interaction surface.

The likely next questions are therefore boundary questions: whether live T3 interaction preserves workspace trust and approval semantics; whether cancellation and streaming remain cooperative and redacted; how model, token-budget, effort, and permission choices stay user-owned at the provider-instance boundary; and whether the Starlark orchestration experiments should remain a separate research lane rather than accrete into the minimal runner core.

The documentation should mirror that architecture: a small durable instruction layer; a few live entry points; a single current handoff per active thread; explicit historical notebooks and archives; and generated browser artifacts clearly labeled as derived. The present repo has the evidence needed for that shape, but its root/current-thread narrative still makes every agent reconstruct the history before finding the current boundary.
